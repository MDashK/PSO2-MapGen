# The usual contents of InfoTable1/2/3, by area.
#
# A zone made by hand has no packet to take these from, and exporting them as zeros
# means fixing the block.json afterwards - these tables appear to drive scenery, so
# a zone with them empty does not look right in game. The values here are defaults,
# measured over the 583 quest packets.
#
# Key: <ModelId> <UnkB> <ZoneIndex>, then one line per table. The lookup falls back
# to <ModelId> <UnkB> and then to <ModelId>, so deleting the finer lines only makes
# the answer coarser. A field is <id>:<value> when its ten values are all the same,
# and <id>:v/v/v/... when they are not.
#
# Coverage of the dominant form, over the corpus: InfoTable2 94%, InfoTable1 86%.
# It is a starting point and not a rule - correct whatever your zone needs.
#
# InfoTable3.Unk1 and Unk2 are NOT here: they are sizeX and sizeY, which the tool
# already knows (1151 of 1151 zones in the corpus). Unk3 and Unk4 decide the map and
# are fields of their own in the window.
#
# The `x =` line is the ZoneSettingsExtra record, same key and same fallback. A zone
# from a packet writes its own values; one made by hand used to export every one of
# these as a zero, and a zero is often not even a value the field ever takes - UnkN
# is 0 in none of the 1641 zones of the corpus. UnkAC and RawE0 are floats stored by
# their raw dword, which is what the files do: 1133903872 is 300.0f. Raw04, Raw10,
# Raw98, RawD4, RawD8, RawDC and RawE0 are named by their offset in the 0xF0 record
# and neither PacketMan nor Starlight writes them yet (FINDINGS 5.4).
